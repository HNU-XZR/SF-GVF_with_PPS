Abstract

The existing singularity-free guiding vector field (SF-GVF) based on an additional virtual coordinate can eliminate the singular points of conventional GVFs and guarantee global convergence of robots to closed and even self-intersecting desired paths. However, the physical speed of the robot along the desired path in the original lower-dimensional space still depends on the path parameterization. In this paper, we show that partially normalizing the physical projection of the SF-GVF, although enabling physical-speed regulation, may introduce new singularities because the normalization denominator can become zero. To address this issue, we propose a new SF-GVF with prescribed physical speed (PPS). The integral curves of the new SF-GVF converge exponentially to the desired path from any initial condition in the higher-dimensional space; more importantly, the robot's physical speed converges to the PPS, while the path-error dynamics remain invariant under regular reparameterizations of the desired path. We further develop a saturated acceleration control law for second-order kinematic models. Finally, the comparative simulation and 3D path-following experiments with a quadrotor under different PPS profiles are conducted to verify the theoretical properties and effectiveness of the proposed approach.
